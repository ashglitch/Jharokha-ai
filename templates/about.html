04 / METHOD NOTES

A useful baseline.
Not a verdict.

JHAROKHA-AI explores a practical research question: can a vision workflow be designed around the visual complexity of heritage surfaces?

01

Pre-process

The photo is resized and contrast-normalised (CLAHE) so lighting differences matter less.

02

Find edges

A trained U-Net marks crack pixels when weights are present; otherwise a ridge filter finds thin dark lines. Thin, elongated shapes are kept. Ornament can still fool it, so review the overlay.

03

Filter & display

The mask is skeletonised to measure length, width, branching and direction. Those feed a condition score, risk tier and re-inspection interval. These are screening heuristics, not engineering values.

THE RESEARCH GAP WE WANT TO TEST

Damage vs. decoration.

Next, build a labelled evaluation set with genuine cracks and hard negatives such as jali openings, carved grooves, mortar joints, scratches and weathered textures. Compare this baseline with a trained model, report precision/recall and false-positive rate, and split data by building—not just by image—to reduce leakage.

Safety noteThis is a student research prototype. It does not estimate structural integrity, crack depth, crack width, urgency, or building safety. Do not use it to make conservation or engineering decisions.
